# Insights

Generated by running `scripts/analyze_insights.py` against the processed
dataset (796 matches, 3 maps, Feb 10–14 2026) and cross-checking the
patterns visually in the tool itself. Raw output is reproducible via:

```bash
scripts/.venv/bin/python3 scripts/analyze_insights.py
```

## 1. Storm deaths are 2–3x more common on Lockdown and GrandRift than on AmbroseValley

**What caught my eye:** Toggling the "storm_deaths" heatmap category
between maps, Lockdown and GrandRift both showed noticeably more purple
storm-death markers relative to their overall match count than
AmbroseValley did.

**Evidence:** Storm deaths as a share of all deaths (Killed + BotKilled +
KilledByStorm):
- AmbroseValley: **3.4%** (17 of 505 deaths)
- Lockdown: **9.2%** (17 of 185 deaths)
- GrandRift: **9.6%** (5 of 52 deaths)

Lockdown and GrandRift players are roughly **2.7x more likely** to die to
the storm than AmbroseValley players, proportionally.

**Actionable:** This points to storm-timing/radius pacing not being
tuned consistently across maps — either the storm closes too
aggressively relative to Lockdown/GrandRift's size, or AmbroseValley's
larger area gives players disproportionately more room to outrun it. The
metric to watch while retuning: storm-death share of total deaths,
targeted toward parity across maps (or an intentional, documented
difference if "smaller map = harsher storm" is the design goal).

**Why a level designer should care:** Storm pacing is one of the few
knobs that directly controls match tension without requiring combat
balance changes. If one map is quietly punishing players for
positioning/rotation more than intended, that shows up as player
frustration ("I died to the circle, not a person") without an obvious
combat-balance root cause — exactly the kind of thing raw telemetry
surfaces before player complaints do.

## 2. GrandRift concentrates kills into a handful of chokepoints; AmbroseValley spreads combat across the whole map

**What caught my eye:** Loading the kills heatmap for GrandRift showed
combat tightly packed around the "EPI" central zone, while the same view
on AmbroseValley showed kills scattered broadly across nearly the whole
playable area.

**Evidence:** Kill-heatmap grid occupancy (100×100 bins):
- AmbroseValley: kills occurred in **751 distinct cells**; the top 10
  hottest cells hold only **7.9%** of all kills — combat is spread thin.
- GrandRift: kills occurred in just **136 distinct cells**; the top 10
  hottest cells hold **18.7%** of all kills — more than double the
  concentration, in a map with far fewer occupied cells to begin with.

**Actionable:** GrandRift's combat funnels into a small number of
locations (visually, the central "EPI" area and a couple of named zones
around it). The actionable lever is loot/objective placement: if
high-value loot or the extraction point pulls everyone toward the same
2-3 zones, fights become repetitive and the rest of the map goes
unused. Metrics to watch after a change: kill-cell occupancy count (want
it to rise toward AmbroseValley's ~750) and top-10-cell kill share (want
it to fall toward AmbroseValley's ~8%).

**Why a level designer should care:** A map where every fight happens in
the same 2-3 spots plays repetitively even if the individual encounters
are fun — players will describe it as "always the same fight" regardless
of combat balance quality. This is also the fastest way to find dead
space: the 86% of GrandRift's grid cells with zero kills are candidates
for redesigned loot routes or landmarks that pull players elsewhere.

## 3. Lockdown — the "close-quarters" map — actually produces the fewest kills per match

**What caught my eye:** The source README describes Lockdown as the
"smaller/close-quarters" map, which reads as an intentional design goal
for tighter, more frequent combat. The data says the opposite happened.

**Evidence:** Average kills per match, and share of matches with zero
kills at all:
- Lockdown: **2.49 avg kills/match**, **28.7%** of matches (49/171) have
  zero kills
- AmbroseValley: **3.18 avg kills/match**, 22.1% zero-kill (125/566)
- GrandRift: **3.27 avg kills/match**, 28.8% zero-kill (17/59)

Lockdown has the *lowest* combat frequency of all three maps despite
being designed for close-quarters engagements, and it also has one of
the lowest average bot counts per match (0.7 vs GrandRift's 0.9), which
likely compounds the effect — fewer opponents present, fewer chances to
fight.

**Actionable:** Two independent levers worth checking: (1) bot
population/spawn density on Lockdown specifically — if it's
under-populated relative to its size, players simply run out matches
without encountering anyone; (2) whether Lockdown's close-quarters
layout is actually enabling *avoidance* (tight sightlines that let
players route around each other) rather than forcing encounters. The
metric to track: avg kills/match and zero-kill-match rate for Lockdown
specifically, watched after any bot-density or layout change.

**Why a level designer should care:** This is a direct mismatch between
design intent ("close-quarters = more fights") and measured outcome
(fewest fights of the three maps). Without telemetry this kind of gap is
invisible — the map can look and feel close-quarters when walking
through it in the editor while still producing the least combat in
actual play.
